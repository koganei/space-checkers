# Space Play design contract

The approved reference is docs/reference.png, option B. This is a native Android app. Board geometry and decoration are drawn at device resolution; pieces use transparent high-resolution master sprites with runtime color shading and native crown overlays.

1. The board is the hero. Use an 8×8 square board with no perspective. Preserve the reference's order: title, opponent badge, board, turn prompt, Undo / Hint / Colors.
2. Space is calm. Background #061A33 with faint blue radial haze, small stars, and blue planets confined to edges. Decoration never crosses board cells or text.
3. Color has a job. Text #F5F8FF; secondary text #A9C1E5; surfaces #142E50 with #36567E borders. Cyan #21D7E9 is the main action. The two teams choose independently from the same named palette, with duplicate colors disallowed. The More action opens the full palette; a color chosen there occupies the first swatch when needed.
4. Pieces are tactile. Layered circular bevels, top-left highlights, low-right shadows. Player 1 men carry a star; player 2 men carry a ring. Kings gain a crown and a second gold rim. Symbols survive color changes.
5. Typography is rounded and warm: bundled Fredoka, weight 600 for headings, 400 for supporting text. Reference headings 36–40 logical units; body 18–20; action labels 18. Never use thin type or all-caps UI copy.
6. Rhythm is shared. 16-unit screen gutters, 24-unit section gaps, 20-unit panel corners, pill-shaped primary buttons. Native Android status/navigation bars remain real, not painted imitations. Content is centered and capped on tablets; short/landscape screens scroll instead of squashing the board.
7. Controls are generous. At least 48dp for non-board touch targets. Every actionable area has a native accessible button, meaningful description, keyboard focus and pressed/focus feedback. Board cells retain their natural square size.
8. New screens use the same app bar, surfaces, buttons, piece previews and orbit language. Rules explanations use plain short sentences. Avoid unrelated Material default cards, neon gradients, reward currencies or menu clutter.
9. Destructive changes are explicit. Rule changes apply only when starting a new game after confirmation. Color changes preserve the live match. Back discards unsaved rule edits. Undo reverses a complete turn (or the current unfinished jump chain).
10. Pixel fidelity is reviewed against the actual screen inside the reference, excluding the mock phone and outer presentation. Reproduce composition, shapes, spacing and materials, while adapting to safe insets and accessible font sizes. Do not claim literal pixel identity across devices.

11. Entry and perspective. Always open on the welcome screen. New Game leads to Play an alien / Two players, then an alien choice when needed, then two large, illustrated player choices; Continue resumes without altering the match. Choosing a side is independent of the rule for who moves first. Keep that chosen side at the bottom for the whole match. Rotate all board rendering, taps, highlights, hints and accessible cell coordinates together; never rotate on turn changes. Save the chosen perspective with the match. Tapping an alien advances immediately; tapping a player starts the match immediately. New Game already expresses replacement intent, so do not ask again.

12. Friendly opponents. Pip is mint with one eye and a sprout; Bloop is blue with two eyes and fins; Ziggy is coral with three eyes and antennae; Luma is lavender with glasses and star antennae. Draw them as rounded native artwork, with consistent highlights, cheek blush and friendly smiles. The opponent list uses four spacious rows with a portrait, name, readable difficulty and one short description. Never communicate difficulty through color alone. Reuse the selected portrait in the game badge.
13. Computer interaction. Clearly label alien turns, prevent human moves during them, and animate jump chains one landing at a time. Search off the UI thread. Navigating away pauses computer play; resuming continues the legal turn. Undo restores the human’s last decision, including the alien reply. Mode/alien selection is a draft until a player is tapped. Never alter an existing match while browsing opponents.

14. Movement is readable. Outline every piece with a legal move using a quiet cyan halo; forced captures and continued jumps use the engine’s legal-turn list. Keep selected-square borders and destination dots distinct from movable-piece outlines. Tap moves and alien moves slide for 240 ms per landing, retaining captured pieces until landing. Dragging lifts the piece slightly, follows the finger beyond board edges, and keeps destination dots visible. An invalid or cancelled drop returns the piece without changing the game. Lock board input during slides; Undo and navigation cancel visual motion safely. Respect the chosen fixed perspective for all gesture coordinates.

15. Sound is gentle and optional. Use short, original rounded space chimes with soft attacks and fades, never harsh buzzers or looping music. Trigger at a successful landing: move or capture, then a crown chime if promoted, then a win fanfare when appropriate. Invalid drops, selection and Undo are silent. The sound toggle is the first settings row, uses the existing switch style, applies immediately and persists separately from game rules. Respect media volume; stop effects and queued cues when leaving play or backgrounding.

16. Finished games offer a clear next step. After the final landing, show a rounded navy popup with a friendly win, loss or draw message, a filled cyan **Play again** button and an outlined **Back to home** button. Replay starts immediately with the same mode, opponent, side, rules, colors and sound preference. Home keeps the finished match saved. Reopening it shows the result again. No extra confirmation follows either action.
